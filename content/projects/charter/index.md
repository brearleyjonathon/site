---
title: Charter
description: Why a designer turned to building performance, and why he thinks the two belong together.
---

Until 2020 most of my work was design. The pandemic changed how I saw the role of buildings in climate and health, and my work moved toward building performance. This charter is how I squared that change with where I started.

Architects, designers and engineers already see themselves as social and political actors. We should also be linchpins in questions of climate and health, and that asks us to stay aware of culture and clear-eyed about the climate ahead, since each now shapes the other. It is a hard brief, and a good one to have.

The discipline usually splits this work up. Some of us are concerned with design, others with social or political practice, others with climate mitigation. I think they are one set of problems. They do not share a single solution, but climate, culture, politics, health, material and structure all act on the same buildings, and any of them is a way into the problems we face together. So the approach has to be holistic, in what we believe and in what we do, and it has to work across these fields toward a more equitable built environment. Process and perspective need to change at every scale, from the house to infrastructure.

The size of these questions is part of what draws me to them, and I am optimistic about them. My career has come to balance advanced building technology and design, and I bring both, with a view of the whole system, to problems that are hard and urgent.
